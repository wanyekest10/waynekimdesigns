## Why dragonflies

Dragonflies are bioindicators. Where they live, the water is clean and oxygen-rich and the banks are healthy, and their multi-year lifecycles link the food chains in the water and on land. As climate change makes water more volatile, designing for dragonflies becomes a way to support the whole ecosystem, not just one species.

<div class="stats">
  <div class="focal"><span class="k">At risk</span><b>16%</b><p>of dragonfly species face extinction from habitat loss, pollution, and climate change</p></div>

## The Astino Valley

Near Bergamo and the CIID campus, the Astino Valley has a network of streams, ponds, and wetlands that support several dragonfly species, including Cordulegaster boltonii. They depend on steady water flow, dense plants along the banks, and consistent oxygen.

Seasonal extremes are breaking that balance. Summer droughts cut the flow and the oxygen. Heavy rain brings flooding, erosion, and lost vegetation.

## Translating ecology into design

We studied how ecosystems handle water under stress, and chose four jobs for the habitat: store, regulate, stabilize, and protect.

## Expert input

With a first concept and prototype, we went back to local ecologists who specialize in Bergamo's fauna. The prototype became a discussion tool for species needs, placement, and long-term impact. They confirmed it had to handle both drought and flooding, and pointed to where an adaptive habitat could do the most good.

> "The approach is very stimulating and worth continuing. We encourage you to develop it further."

## Concept

A modular habitat that responds to both drought and flooding.

<figure class="frame"><img src="/work/larvae-assets/concept.webp" alt="3D render of the habitat concept: a floating ring with petal-like edges, a sheltered pool in the middle, and an anchor line below"/></figure>
 It holds water and keeps it oxygen-rich in dry spells, stays put in floods, and adapts to different sites and species. Being modular means it can be reconfigured instead of optimized for one scenario.

## Reflection

**Longer horizons.** Designing for non-human users means longer timelines and different measures of success.

**Systems reveal dependencies.** Ecological systems thinking showed connections that made the solution more resilient.

**Work with the experts.** Biologists and ecologists made our design decisions stronger.
