## The closet

I needed a real place to work after deciding to build out a breakfast nook and not wanting my work station to be in my bedroom. My den closet was 67 inches wide and empty. I'm also learning SolidWorks at LACI as a Product Design Fellow and halfway through the program, so the desk became my practice project.

## The model

One master part holds the wood. Save Bodies splits it into parts and an assembly, and the hardware goes in with mates.

<div class="flow" role="list" aria-label="SolidWorks workflow">
  <div class="step"><span class="n">01</span><b>Master part</b><small>Top, aprons, and legs as bodies.</small></div>
  <span class="to" aria-hidden="true">→</span>
  <div class="step"><span class="n">02</span><b>Assembly</b><small>Save Bodies into linked parts.</small></div>
  <span class="to" aria-hidden="true">→</span>
  <div class="step"><span class="n">03</span><b>Hardware</b><small>The real brace, placed with mates.</small></div>
  <span class="to" aria-hidden="true">→</span>
  <div class="step focal"><span class="n">04</span><b>The wall</b><small>Holes and braces patterned at the studs.</small></div>
</div>

The desk used to live where my breakfast nook is now, and now that space is free again. I'm halfway through the LACI product design fellowship, and it was a good opportunity to model, design, and actually build.

Next, I'll keep building out this closet office as a workbench for 3D printing, soldering, and tinkering.
