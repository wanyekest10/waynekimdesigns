## The approach

A workshop only pays off if someone turns the wall into a plan. Every session I run follows the same arc.

<div class="flow" role="list" aria-label="How I run a workshop">
  <div class="step"><span class="n">01</span><b>Separate certainty from assumption</b><small>Before any activity, agree on what we know.</small></div>
  <span class="to" aria-hidden="true">→</span>
  <div class="step"><span class="n">02</span><b>Write first, talk second</b><small>Everyone puts a position down before the group discusses.</small></div>
  <span class="to" aria-hidden="true">→</span>
  <div class="step focal"><span class="n">03</span><b>Turn the wall into a plan</b><small>Synthesize the room into questions and next steps.</small></div>
</div>
<p class="flow-note">↻ The plan becomes the starting point for the next round of research.</p>

## Glorify, in full

By late 2023, Glorify's leadership had strong opinions about where the core experience should go next, but those opinions hadn't been tested against each other. I designed and facilitated a workshop with senior leadership across Content, Design, Product, and Research, then spent the following weeks turning what came out of the room into prioritized research questions for the year ahead.

### The design

The workshop had one job before it had any activities: separate certainty from assumption. I framed the session around three objectives.

<div class="cols3">
  <div><h4>Shared language</h4><p>For the group's opinions, and the reasoning behind them.</p></div>
  <div><h4>Certainty vs. assumption</h4><p>Separate what the team knew from what it was assuming.</p></div>
  <div><h4>Inform the work</h4><p>Use both to shape strategy, research, and tactics.</p></div>
</div>

Three time-boxed activities anchored the session: what to double down on, what's missing, and how to personalize the experience.

### In the room

Senior stakeholders sharing a point of view is different from sharing one that can be acted on. Participants worked through each prompt on their own before discussing as a group, so the conversation started from written positions instead of whoever spoke first. The room produced a wall of perspectives on strengths, gaps, and personalization, plus a transcript of where people agreed, where they didn't, and why.

### What came out of it

I synthesized the discussion into four themes for the year ahead. One traced a personalization assumption through four hypotheses to two research questions. Another narrowed three competing explanations for why the Listen Tab was underused into a single testable question.

## Reflection

**The synthesis is the deliverable.** The room is where opinions come out. The weeks after are where they turn into something a team can act on.

**Structure is what makes it safe.** Writing before talking means the quietest person in the room is on the wall too.
